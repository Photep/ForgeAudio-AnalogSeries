<objective>
Land the real pitch chain and the hostile-input bound that FM makes necessary.

Today the pitch is one term: the V/OCT volts alone. This plan widens that single expression into
the four-term volt-domain sum PITCH-01/02/03 and FM-01/02/03 require, gated on `fmConnected`,
bounded before the exponential, and still passing through exactly ONE `forge::exp2_taylor5` call.
Everything downstream of that call — the sanitised rate, the ceiling, the negated floor, the
telemetry write, the double accumulator, the wrap, the waveshape call and the unconditioned x5 —
is untouched.

The bound is a fix, and this project has twice rejected a fix whose failing case was never
observed. Research MEASURED that a behavioral RED case here is VACUOUS: today's core already
survives NaN, +/-inf, +/-1e30 and +/-200 V pitch with a finite output, a zero `tel.freqHz` and
`|out| <= 5 V`, because the existing negated frequency floor catches the garbage the undefined
behavior produces. So D-22 fixes the evidence tier: the RED is a one-shot UBSan probe that names
both frozen UB sites by line, recorded with its literal diagnostic text, and the permanent
suite check is a standing behavioral case pinned at the bound (31-07's, not this plan's).

Purpose: the pitch chain becomes real, and the undefined behavior FM would newly reach is closed
with evidence that actually went red.
Output: one new constant, four new lines of arithmetic, and a recorded RED-then-clean sanitizer
transcript.
</objective>

**The constant.** Add `constexpr float kVcoMaxPitchVolts = 64.f;` to the existing constants
block inside `namespace forge`, as a namespace-scope plain `constexpr float` — the same idiom as
its neighbours. Place it after the Nyquist guard fraction and before the phase-increment bound so
the two frequency-domain constants stay adjacent. Never an inline variable, never an in-class
static member: under C++11 the in-class form is a declaration only, runtime indexing odr-uses it,
and MinGW then fails to link. That exact construct got v2.0.0 rejected from the VCV Library.

Give it a whole-line comment block recording, in the house style:

  - what it guards: the frozen `forge::exp2Floor` casts its argument plus 127 to `int32_t` and
    then shifts that integer left by 23 into a float union. Casting a non-finite or
    out-of-`int32_t` float is undefined behavior, and shifting a negative or over-large integer
    is undefined behavior as well. Rack does not sanitise cable voltages, and FM is the term that
    newly puts one of them into the exponent argument;
  - why the fix is here and not there: the frozen header is byte-pinned by `tests/check_frozen.sh`
    and is consumed by the SHIPPED LFO, so editing it is a guardrail event rather than a VCO fix;
  - the measured envelope: the frozen helper is well defined only while its argument lands in
    `[-127, +128]`;
  - the measured reachable musical worst case: about 29.08 V, from Rack's +/-12 V cable norm on
    V/OCT plus +/-5 octaves of COARSE plus one twelfth of an octave of FINE plus +/-12 V of FM at
    1.0 octave per volt. 64 therefore sits about 2.2x outside anything a legitimate patch can
    produce and about 2.0x inside the undefined-behavior boundary;
  - why 64 rather than a neighbour: it is a power of two and therefore exactly representable, so
    the comparisons carry no rounding, and the resulting frequency stays FINITE at both extremes
    (measured 4.826e21 at the top and 1.418e-17 at the bottom) where a bound of 120 or 126 would
    produce a positive infinity at the top and hand the downstream ceiling an infinity instead of
    a real number;
  - the consequence at the negative bound, which is deliberate and consistent with D-13: the
    frequency becomes denormal-small, the existing negated floor does not fire because the value
    is positive, the increment is about 3.2e-22, and the output is effectively DC.

**The pitch block.** Replace the single-line pitch expression with the four-step sequence, in
this exact order, immediately before the existing sanitised-rate line:

  1. `float pitchVolts = in.pitchCV + in.coarse + in.fine * (1.f / 12.f);`
  2. `if (in.fmConnected) pitchVolts += in.fmVolts * in.fmAtten;`
  3. `if (!(pitchVolts > -kVcoMaxPitchVolts)) pitchVolts = -kVcoMaxPitchVolts;` followed by
     `if (pitchVolts > kVcoMaxPitchVolts) pitchVolts = kVcoMaxPitchVolts;`
  4. `float freq = kVcoFreqC4 * exp2_taylor5(pitchVolts);`

The negated comparison comes FIRST and is the NaN catcher: a NaN fails `> -kVcoMaxPitchVolts`, so
it lands on the fallback branch and becomes the negative bound, which then fails the plain upper
comparison and survives. Record that reasoning in a comment, and record that `forge::clamp` is
rejected here by name because both of its comparisons are false for a NaN so the value passes
through unchanged — inert against exactly the input class this guard exists to stop.

Keep everything in float. The summation lives entirely upstream of the `(double)freq` cast and
the measured float summation error is 0.0011 cents; promoting it to double would diverge from the
float-typed POD fields for no measurable gain and would put a needless conversion in the audio
path.

Extend the pitch call site's comment to state: that the four terms are summed in the volt domain
and exponentiated exactly ONCE, which is what makes the FM musical exponential FM; that
multiplying frequencies or calling the exponential twice produces linear-FM behavior or
per-octave detuning and is the pitfall this ordering exists to avoid; that the SHIPPED LFO's FM
path is the counter-example and only its connected-gate is borrowed; that full clockwise on the
attenuverter is 1.0 octave per volt by decision, so the FM jack at full depth is a second V/OCT

the semitone-to-octave division because the POD's field is documented in semitones and the shell
stays a pure forwarder; and that the bound must sit between the summation and the exponential
because it exists to protect the cast the exponential reaches.

The FM contribution carries NO named depth constant. A constant equal to one would be a multiply
the compiler removes and a number with no requirement behind it; the 1.0 octave-per-volt contract
lives in the comment, where it can be read.
