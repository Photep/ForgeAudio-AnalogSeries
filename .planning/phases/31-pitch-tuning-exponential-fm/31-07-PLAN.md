<objective>
Close the two invariants the existing suite cannot cover, and put the other file's stand-in back in
step with the core it mirrors.

The existing suite already pins the Nyquist bound symbolically — but it does so from hostile TIMING,
and it never observes the clamp firing on a legitimate high note, nor that the oscillator is still
audible when it does. That distinction matters because the clamp is a timbral decision: under deep
FM it fires on most cycles and the chosen sound is peaks flattening out, not silence. So invariant 8
drives real pitch above the derived ceiling, asserts the ceiling was applied exactly, and asserts the
oscillator is still oscillating.

Invariant 9 is the standing half of the hostile-input work. Its RED counterpart was a one-shot
sanitizer probe, because research MEASURED that a behavioral case here would have been green before
the guard existed. This case is therefore explicitly the permanent regression check rather than the
evidence the guard was needed — and it carries one thing the sanitizer cannot express: a symbolic
assertion, computed from the declared control ranges, that the worst sum a legitimate patch can
produce sits a factor of two inside the bound. That property is not observable through the output at
all, because the Nyquist ceiling dominates at those volts, which is exactly why the bound is
inaudible and exactly why the evidence for it has to be arithmetic.

Finally, the stand-in core in the other test file carries a banner promising it is mirrored from the
real core in everything except one deliberate defect. The real core just changed. Keeping that
promise is a small edit; not keeping it turns the banner into a false comment.

Purpose: PITCH-04's decided behavior and D-14's bound both have permanent, non-vacuous coverage, and
no test-file comment outlives its truth.
Output: two invariants, one compile-time assertion, one mirror brought up to date with its figures
re-observed.
</objective>
