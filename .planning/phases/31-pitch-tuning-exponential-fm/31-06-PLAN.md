<objective>
Prove the three controls the shell just gained actually do what their requirements say, and prove
the FM path sums rather than multiplies.

The COARSE and FINE cases are straightforward measurements with one trap each. For COARSE the trap
is that an integer-only grid cannot distinguish a continuous sweep from a snapped one, so
non-integer values are part of the grid by requirement rather than by thoroughness. For FINE the
trap is that the assertion has to pin the DIVISOR: a hundred-cent shift at one semitone is the only
number that distinguishes the correct conversion from no conversion at all and from a wrong one.

The FM case has a much sharper trap, and it decides whether this phase's central requirement is
actually tested. FM-03 says the FM voltage sums into the volt domain BEFORE the single exponential,
and the natural test is that a static FM voltage equals the same voltage on V/OCT. A MULTIPLICATIVE
implementation — the shipped LFO's shape, which is the explicit counter-example — satisfies that
identity EXACTLY at integer volt sums, because the frozen polynomial produces integer octaves by
writing the floating-point exponent field and is therefore bit-exact there. An integer-only grid
would pass the wrong implementation. So the grid carries fractional volt pairs, and a stand-in core
that multiplies is required to fail on them, driven through the same helper the real check uses.

Purpose: the requirements are measured rather than asserted, and the central one has a control that
demonstrably fires.
Output: four invariants, one templated drive helper and one deliberately broken core.
</objective>
