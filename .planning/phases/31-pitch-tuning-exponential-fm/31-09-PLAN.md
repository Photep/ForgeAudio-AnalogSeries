  <what-built>
The pitch chain and its four new controls.

`forge::VcoCore` now sums the V/OCT input, the COARSE knob in octaves, the FINE knob converted from
semitones, and the FM jack scaled by its bipolar depth control — all in the volt domain, all through
exactly one exponential off the C4 reference. The Nyquist policy is settled and the summed pitch
volts are bounded before the exponential so a hostile cable voltage cannot reach the frozen
float-to-int cast. `src/AnalogVCO.cpp` declares COARSE, FINE, FM DEPTH and an FM input jack and
forwards them as bare assignments; it still performs no arithmetic. The throwaway panel gained four
marker squares.

The headless proof is nine new test cases: tracking better than one cent measured on the OUTPUT
against an independent reference at three sample rates, coarse and fine range and continuity, the FM
summation identity proven bit-exact with a multiplicative stand-in required to FAIL it, the clamp
proven to fire while the oscillator keeps sounding, and a standing hostile-pitch case pinned at the
bound. All four local gates and the three-operating-system matrix including the Windows link leg are
green on this commit.
  </what-built>
  <expected-results>
**Read all of this before answering. Every item below is the DECIDED behavior for this phase, not a
defect — reporting one as a bug is fine, but none of them is a surprise.**

1. **The timbre is crude, buzzy and aliased ON PURPOSE.** There is no band-limiting yet; that is the
   next phase. High notes will sound harsh and grainy. Do not judge this module on sound quality.
2. **The output is unconditioned and can exceed five volts** at high CHARACTER — the measured ceiling
   is about 5.55 volts, well inside Rack's normal range. No direct-current blocker, no saturation, no
   output clamp. The output stage is a later phase.
3. **The panel is a deliberately ugly placeholder:** a dark rectangle, an orange bar and eight
   unlabelled grey squares at the final eighteen-HP size. Nothing is labelled and nothing is styled.
   The real panel is a later phase. The FM depth control is a full knob for now; whether it becomes a
   scalloped trimpot is that phase's decision.
4. **Tooltips read with five significant digits, not two decimals.** Expect something like
   `+2.0000 oct`, `-14.000 cents` and `-100.00%`. The UNITS are what this phase fixed — coarse in
   octaves, fine in cents, depth as a percentage. The digit count was left at the default, matching
   the shipped Analog LFO, which sets none anywhere.
5. **Under deep FM the peaks FLATTEN at the top rather than going silent.** The frequency pins just
   below Nyquist — about 21.8 kHz at 44.1 kHz — and stays there. With one octave per volt, a
   five-volt audio-rate modulator swings five octaves, so the ceiling is reached constantly and that
   flattening is the chosen sound. An amplitude fade was considered and rejected because it collides
   with the later output stage.
6. **At extreme negative pitch the oscillator stalls to a near-constant output.** There is
   deliberately no low-end floor: a VCO tuned absurdly low genuinely is direct current, and you asked
   for it.
7. **COARSE is continuous with no snap of any kind,** by decision. Control-click or command-click
   returns it to exactly zero, which is how you get back to concert pitch. An octave-snap menu is
   deferred.
8. **FM DEPTH is bipolar.** Fully clockwise is one octave per volt — the jack behaves exactly like a
   second V/OCT input at that setting. Fully counter-clockwise inverts the modulation. Centre is off.
9. **Every Analog VCO you add to a patch is a bit-identical clone of the others** — the per-instance
   seed is still hardcoded. That is a recorded deferred item for a later phase, not something to fix
   here.
10. **The shipped Analog LFO must be completely unchanged.** Nothing in this phase touched its source,
    its shared headers or its registration, and its bit-exact fixtures replay identically in the
    suite. If it looks or sounds different in this session, that is the most important thing you could
    report.
  </expected-results>
  <how-to-verify>
Rack was closed for the install, so start it fresh.

1. **It appears.** Open the module browser and add **Analog VCO**. Confirm **Analog LFO** is still
   there beside it and still opens normally.
2. **V/OCT tracks.** Patch a keyboard or sequencer into **V/OCT** and the **audio output** into your
   interface. Play octaves. Confirm each octave up is heard as an octave, across as much of the
   keyboard as your source covers. Concert-pitch A should sound like concert-pitch A against any
   reference you trust.
3. **COARSE sweeps five octaves each way, continuously.** With nothing patched into V/OCT, sweep
   **COARSE** from fully counter-clockwise to fully clockwise. Confirm roughly ten octaves of travel,
   confirm it glides rather than stepping, and confirm the tooltip reads in octaves. Then
   control-click or command-click it and confirm it returns to exactly zero and to concert pitch.
4. **FINE trims about a semitone.** Add a second Analog VCO (or any oscillator) at the same pitch.
   Sweep **FINE** on one of them. Confirm a slow beat that speeds up as you move away from centre,
   confirm the tooltip reads in cents, and confirm the extremes are around a hundred cents — a
   semitone — rather than a tone.
5. **One volt of FM is one octave.** Patch an LFO into the **FM** jack and set **FM DEPTH** fully
   clockwise. With a one-volt modulation you should hear roughly an octave of vibrato. Turn FM DEPTH
   to centre and confirm the vibrato stops completely. Turn it fully counter-clockwise and confirm the
   vibrato returns INVERTED — the pitch goes down where it previously went up.
6. **Audio-rate FM sounds like exponential FM.** Patch an audio-rate oscillator into **FM** and bring
   **FM DEPTH** up. Confirm you get the clangorous, metallic, harmonically dense sound of exponential
   FM rather than silence, a click or a dropout. Push the depth to full and confirm the peaks flatten
   out at the top — item 5 in the expected results — rather than going silent.
7. **Unpatch the FM jack.** Confirm the pitch returns exactly to where the tune controls put it, with
   no residual modulation and no jump.
8. **Extremes do not break anything.** Take COARSE to each extreme with FM at full depth and confirm
   Rack does not hang, stutter, crash or fall silent, and that nothing downstream of the VCO gets
   poisoned. Save and reload the patch and confirm it opens without hanging.
9. **The LFO is untouched.** In the same session, run the Analog LFO through whatever you normally
   use to judge it. Confirm it looks identical and sounds identical.

If anything differs from the ten expected results above, or if anything in steps 1 to 9 does not
behave as described, say what you observed. Partial answers are fine — say which steps you did.
